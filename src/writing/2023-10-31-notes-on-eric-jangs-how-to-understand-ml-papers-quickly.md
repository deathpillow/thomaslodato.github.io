<p>I found <a href="https://evjang.com/2021/01/25/understanding-ml.html">&ldquo;How to Understand ML Papers Quickly&rdquo;</a> by Eric Jang (VP @ 1X, ex-Google) recently in my learning about generative AI. Written in 2021, I wonder what might be updated about these points given the state of knowledge currently.</p>
<p>Here is my summary of the already-pretty-concise original post:</p>
<ul>
<li>Determine the inputs &amp; outputs of the ML problem &amp; whether these inputs <em>can even</em> produce these outputs</li>
<li>&ldquo;ML models are formed from combining biases and data&rdquo; so figure out which is being added/subtracted</li>
<li>Determine to what extent the model be generalized &amp; whether it requires learning (as opposed to hard-coding) to get there</li>
<li>Make sure the claims can be falsified (is it science?)</li>
</ul>
